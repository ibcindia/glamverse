The Runway: Where Fashion Becomes a Story

By the GLAMVERSE team · 6 minute read

For about twenty minutes, a long, narrow platform becomes the most important street in the world. Lights drop, music starts, and a designer's year of work walks past an audience in a few steps at a time. That is the runway, and it is much more than a place to show clothes.

From private salon to global stage

Fashion was not always a public show. In nineteenth-century Paris, designers such as Charles Frederick Worth are often credited with presenting clothes on live models to wealthy clients in private salons. Showings were small, quiet and by invitation only.

Over the twentieth century the salons grew into scheduled seasonal events, and the catwalk took the shape we know today. Fashion weeks in New York, London, Milan and Paris became the four calendar anchors the industry still plans around. In India, fashion weeks in Mumbai and Delhi have grown since the early 2000s into major platforms for both established labels and new designers.

What a runway show is really for

A collection is a set of ideas, and a show is how a designer explains them. Every part of it carries meaning:

  • The order of looks. Designers build a rhythm, usually opening with a strong statement and finishing with the most dramatic piece.
  • The casting. The people chosen to walk tell you who the designer imagines wearing the clothes.
  • The set and sound. A mirrored hall, a muddy field or a quiet white room all change how the same coat feels.
  • The styling. Shoes, bags, hair and makeup show how the pieces are meant to be worn together.
The best shows leave you remembering a feeling first and a hemline second.

How a show reaches your wardrobe

Buyers and editors attend shows to decide what will appear in stores and magazines. Pieces first seen on the runway are then produced, shipped and sold months later, which is why the clothes you see in a spring show are usually in shops by the following season. Ideas travel even faster: a color, a shape or a styling trick can show up in street style and high-street stores within weeks.

A bigger audience than ever

Live streaming and social media have opened the front row to everyone. A viewer at home can now watch a show as it happens, see backstage preparation and react in real time. Designers have started to build shows with the camera in mind, so a good look now has to work in a hall and on a phone screen.

How to watch your next show

  • Watch the whole show once without pausing, to catch the mood.
  • Watch it again and note the three looks you would actually wear.
  • Look at the shoes and accessories, since these often predict the next trends.
  • Read what the designer says afterwards. It often changes how you see the collection.
